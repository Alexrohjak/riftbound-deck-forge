The workbench

A clickable prototype of the three things D2 has to settle: how the workbench works, how EE speaks, and how EE is invoked. Built phone-first, with real cards from the cached gallery.

The thesis: the interface is greyscale, so colour always means domain. Riftbound already has a six-colour language, and Domain Identity is the most constraining rule in deckbuilding. If Forge spends colour on itself, it competes with the only colour that carries meaning. Legality is therefore encoded in form — weight, strike, dashed rule — not in red and green, which would collide with Fury and Body anyway.

Ownership is the headline, not a detail

The bar reads names and free copies — not "1,180 cards". Every other tool counts the whole game; Forge counts what you can actually sleeve tonight.

Pips: filled means available

Each pip is one copy you own. Filled = free to use. Hollow = committed to a BUILT deck. A card with no free copies dims but never disappears — you still need to see it to know why you can't have it.

This is the piece no existing tool has, because no existing tool models a collection.

3

Legality is carried by form

A solid underline means the zone is satisfied. A dashed one means short, with the deficit beside it. Over-limit strikes through. Colour is never the signal — it's reserved for domain, and green/red would collide with Body and Fury.

Counts are always have/need, never a percentage or a grade.

D-016 · no composite score

4

The deck never leaves the screen

On the phone the deck is a dock you drag up, so browsing and building are the same activity. On desktop the identical component becomes the right-hand panel — same state, same counts. Full parity, including editing.

D-018 · phone has full parity

5

Ask is a button, never a notification

EE has no badge, no live warnings, no "3 suggestions". It is silent until pressed. Tapping a card carries that card into the question, so you never type context you've already given.

Answers come as a statement, a lever you can act on, and its grounding — with facts and estimates visually incapable of being confused.

What this prototype decides — and what it doesn't

Settled here

Ownership visual language · zone rail and legality-by-form · dock-to-panel parity · EE's three-part answer shape · the invocation model.

Deliberately not settled

Collection entry mode (W2's keyboard flow) and the sample-hand simulator. Both are real screens, but neither changes the decisions above — and designing them now would sprawl D2.

Needs your eye

Whether the pips read instantly or need numbers; whether the dock is the right phone metaphor; and whether EE's answer is the right length. That last one becomes S3's statement budget.

Cards, costs and rules text are real, taken from the cached Riot gallery (1,180 printings). Ownership counts and deck state are illustrative — the real collection is entered at W2. No logic here is production code; DESIGN LOCKED still gates that until D3.
